# Measuring Biological Capabilities and Risks of AI Agents

Published: June 2026 | Authors: Patricia Paskov, Jeffrey Lee, Kyle Brady, Alyssa Worland (RAND Corporation)
arXiv: 2606.19899 | RAND Report: PEA4710-1

## Overview
This perspective addresses how to generate and interpret credible evidence about the biological capabilities and risks of AI scientists — agentic AI systems capable of autonomously or collaboratively performing multi-step scientific tasks. As these systems enter real research workflows, decision-makers face evaluation results whose meaning depends on underlying design choices that are often implicit or under-documented.

## Core Contribution
A set of practical, experience-grounded considerations showing how choices around defining, designing, running, scoring, and documenting evaluations materially shape what results do and do not imply about risk.

## Five Practical Considerations
1. **Defining** what the evaluation is meant to measure
2. **Translating** goals into tasks and measurement instruments
3. **Running** the evaluation under specific operational conditions
4. **Scoring** the evaluation
5. **Documenting** and communicating results responsibly

## Target Audiences
- Policymakers interpreting biological evaluation outputs
- Public and private funders of AI-biology evaluation research
- Biosecurity practitioners assessing emerging AI systems
- Researchers designing agentic evaluations within frontier AI labs

## Framework
Introduces "biological agentic evaluations" as a tool for assessing AI systems' biological capabilities, while emphasizing that results are highly interpretation-sensitive. The paper draws from the authors' own evaluation experience at RAND's Center on AI, Security, and Technology.
