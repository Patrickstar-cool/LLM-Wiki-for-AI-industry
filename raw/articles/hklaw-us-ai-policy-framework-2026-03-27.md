# White House Releases a National Policy Framework for AI

Holland & Knight Alert | March 27, 2026

The White House released a National Policy Framework for AI on March 20, 2026 — nonbinding legislative recommendations to guide Congress on federal AI legislation.

## Background

Executive Order 14365 (Dec 11, 2025) initiated a coordinated federal review of state-level AI laws and established an AI Litigation Task Force to challenge unconstitutional or preempted state measures. The Commerce Department's required evaluation of "onerous" state laws was due March 11, 2026 but has not been publicly released.

## Seven Priority Areas

1. Protecting children and empowering parents
2. Safeguarding and strengthening communities
3. Respecting IP and supporting creators
4. Preventing censorship and protecting free speech
5. Enabling innovation and US AI dominance
6. Educating an AI-ready workforce
7. Federal policy framework with targeted preemption of state AI laws

## Key Positions

- Training AI on copyrighted material does not violate copyright (Trump admin view); supports voluntary licensing
- Bar federal agencies from pressuring AI providers to suppress lawful content
- Codify ratepayer protection: tech companies must pay for electricity used by AI data centers
- Targeted federal preemption of state AI laws for national uniformity

## TRUMP AMERICA AI Act

Sen. Marsha Blackburn's 291-page updated draft (March 18, 2026) covers 17 titles: developer duties of care, liability, labor reporting, product liability, advanced AI risk evaluation, content provenance.

## State-Level Activity

Colorado, California, Texas, and Illinois have enacted broad AI governance statutes. EU AI Act compliance deadline approaching (August 2026), with final Transparency Code of Practice published.
