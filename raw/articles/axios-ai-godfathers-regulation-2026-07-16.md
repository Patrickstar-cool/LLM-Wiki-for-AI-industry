# Behind the Curtain: AI godfathers converge on regulations

By Jim VandeHei, Mike Allen | Axios | July 16, 2026

The three men racing hardest to build superhuman AI — Demis Hassabis (Google DeepMind), Sam Altman (OpenAI), and Dario Amodei (Anthropic) — all agree the frontier needs to be regulated ASAP. For the first time, the three CEOs are on the record converging on the same diagnosis and remarkably similar prescriptions.

## Five Areas of Convergence

1. **Independent testing**: Frontier models subject to outside scrutiny before public release
2. **One governing system**: Bodies that set standards, certify compliance, limit access to dangerous systems
3. **America First**: US should set terms for an internationally-reaching body, not fragmented state or international rules
4. **Threat awareness**: Imminent national security risks from cyber and bioweapon capabilities
5. **Innovation protection**: Only target the small class of frontier models capable of catastrophic risk

## Three Models, Three Philosophies

- **Amodei (Anthropic)**: "FAA for AI" — federal agency with power to block release immediately from Day 1
- **Hassabis (Google DeepMind)**: "FINRA for AI" — industry-funded, federally overseen standards body; voluntary → mandatory
- **Altman (OpenAI)**: "IAEA for AI" — US-led international forum certifying countries and companies, using market access as leverage

## Risks

Critics fear regulatory capture: rules written to make AI safer may entrench the biggest AI companies. Startups and open-source developers face steeper compliance climbs.

## Context

The convergence comes during an extraordinary stretch where Washington twice intervened to restrict or delay frontier models (Anthropic's Fable/Mythos in June, OpenAI's GPT-5.6 in July). Meta's Zuckerberg is reportedly working on his own memo.
